1. Business Requirements
   1. Background
Students and working professionals preparing for English proficiency tests (especially IELTS-like exams) currently use a fragmented mix of resources: printed books, random online mock tests, YouTube videos, and ad-hoc vocabulary apps. Their practice history and scores are scattered across platforms, making it difficult to track progress or understand strengths and weaknesses.
Teachers and academic coordinators maintain exam banks, courses, and vocabulary using spreadsheets or isolated tools. They spend significant time manually updating content, tracking learner progress, and consolidating results before each testing period.
There is no unified platform where learners can (a) register accounts securely, (b) take realistic mock exams with structured sections and time limits, (c) enroll in structured courses with lesson-level progress tracking, and (d) review vocabulary using spaced-repetition decks, all while admins can manage content and monitor attempts through a single system. 
   2. Business Opportunity
A web-based platform that combines mock exam delivery, structured courses, and vocabulary decks into a single integrated environment would:
* Help learners practice in an exam-like environment and receive clear feedback on their performance.
* Allow academic staff to manage exam catalogs, course content, and vocabulary centrally instead of using scattered tools.
* Provide detailed attempt logs and progress statistics, enabling data-driven teaching decisions and targeted remediation.
* Reduce the time admins spend on manual result compilation and basic technical support (e.g., account creation, password resets) by automating registration, OTP-based verification, login, and session management.
   1. Business Objectives
BO-1: Improve learner exam readiness
* Description: Increase the proportion of active learners who reach or exceed their target band in internal mock exams.
* Scale: Percentage of active learners achieving target band (as defined in their profile) in at least one mock exam per term.
* Meter: Analysis of stored exam attempts and computed band scores from the attempt service.
* Past: 25% (baseline from pre-platform pilot).
* Goal: ≥ 40% within 12 months after initial release.
* Stretch: ≥ 50% within 18 months.
BO-2: Increase practice engagement
* Description: Increase the average number of completed exam attempts and flashcard review sessions per active learner.
* Scale: Average number of completed exam attempts plus flashcard review sessions per active learner per month.
* Meter: System logs of exam attempts and vocabulary review sessions.
* Past: 1 mock exam and 0 structured review sessions per month (estimated before platform).
* Goal: 3 combined sessions per month within 6 months.
* Stretch: 4+ combined sessions per month within 12 months.
BO-3: Reduce administrative workload for academic staff
* Description: Decrease the time staff spend managing exam content, courses, vocabulary, and manual reporting.
* Scale: Average hours per week spent on manual content management and reporting by admin staff.
* Meter: Time-tracking survey and periodic internal interviews.
* Past: 10 hours/week (manual spreadsheets, ad-hoc tools).
* Goal: ≤ 5 hours/week within 6 months after release 1.
* Stretch: ≤ 3 hours/week with analytics and export automation in later releases.
   1. Success Metrics
SM-1: At least 70% of users who register and verify their account take at least one exam attempt within 30 days of registration, within 6 months following initial release. 
SM-2: The average rating on a quarterly learner satisfaction survey about exam practice and course experience increases by 0.5 on a 1–6 scale within 6 months after release 1 and by 1.0 within 12 months.
SM-3: At least 80% of course-enrolled learners show a course progress completion rate of 60% or higher by the end of each term, measured by completed lessons over mandatory lessons.
   2. Vision Statement
For learners who need structured English practice, realistic mock exams, and systematic vocabulary review, Langfens English is a web-based exam and learning platform that lets admins manage exam catalogs, courses, and vocabulary decks while learners securely register, take timed exam attempts, enroll in courses, track progress, and review flashcards. Unlike fragmented resources such as standalone mock-test sites, spreadsheets, and generic vocabulary apps, Langfens English provides an integrated environment with secure OTP-based account verification, consistent exam delivery snapshots, and detailed attempt history and progress tracking for both learners and academic staff.
   3. Business Risks
RI-1: Insufficient content production (exams, courses, vocabulary decks) might reduce learner engagement and usage of the platform.
* Probability = 0.5; Impact = 7
RI-2: Low adoption by learners (e.g., they continue using existing external resources) could reduce the perceived return on investment of the platform.
* Probability = 0.4; Impact = 8
RI-3: Security or privacy incidents related to account management, OTP handling, or exam data could damage trust and force the institution to suspend the system. 
* Probability = 0.2; Impact = 9
RI-4: Infrastructure limitations (e.g., unstable network, insufficient server capacity during peak exam times) might cause exam interruptions or lost attempts, harming user satisfaction.
* Probability = 0.5; Impact = 8
   1. Business Assumptions and Dependencies
AS-1: Reliable email infrastructure is available for sending OTPs and system notifications for registration and password reset.
AS-2: Google OAuth and other authentication providers remain available and properly configured for social login. 
AS-3: Academic staff will provide and maintain exam definitions, course content, and vocabulary decks according to an agreed schedule.
AS-4: Learners have basic access to the Internet and modern browsers compatible with the platform requirements.
DE-1: The platform depends on internal services (Exam Catalog Service, Attempt Service, Vocabulary Service, Course Service) for correct exam delivery, scoring, and spaced-repetition scheduling.
DE-2: Data protection and institutional IT policies must be followed for storage of attempts, personal information, and media (speaking recordings, writing answers).
2. Scope and Limitations
   1. Major Features
FE-1: Exam catalog and structure management
Admins manage the exam catalog (categories, metadata, states Draft/Published/Archived) and define exam structures including sections, questions, scoring rules, and special speaking/writing tasks.
FE-2: Exam delivery and attempt lifecycle
Candidates start timed exam attempts, the system creates immutable delivery snapshots, autosaves responses, enforces time limits, calculates scores, and stores attempt histories for review. Internal services fetch delivery snapshots for scoring and review.
FE-3: Account registration, authentication, and session management
Users register and verify accounts via email OTP, log in using password or Google, maintain active sessions, and reset passwords via email OTP under security and rate-limiting rules.
FE-4: Course and lesson management with learner progress tracking
Admins manage courses and lesson structures; learners enroll in published courses and the system tracks lesson completion and course progress percentages.
FE-5: Vocabulary decks and flashcard review
Admins or creators manage vocabulary decks and cards. Learners and public users browse published decks, while authenticated learners subscribe and perform spaced-repetition flashcard reviews with performance statistics.
FE-6: Public browsing of exams, courses, and vocabulary
Visitors and public users (unauthenticated) browse published exams, courses, and vocabulary decks in read-only mode, which supports marketing and discovery.
FE-7: Admin monitoring and reporting
Admins view and monitor ongoing and completed attempts, assign graders for speaking/writing tasks, and export results for analysis.
All features above are realized strictly through the defined use cases for Admin, End User, and Internal Services.

	   3. Limitations and Exclusions
LI-1: The platform will not serve as an official high-stakes examination system for external certification bodies; it is focused on practice and internal assessment.
LI-2: Offline exam taking (without any Internet connectivity) is out of scope for all planned releases.
LI-3: Payment processing for paid exams or courses is not part of release 1; if implemented later, it will be covered by separate use cases and integrations.
LI-4: Third-party proctoring (e.g., webcam monitoring, AI-based cheating detection) is not included in initial scope.

	   3. Deployment Considerations
The platform will initially be deployed as a web application accessible via modern browsers on desktop and mobile devices. The server environment must support secure HTTPS connections, database storage for user accounts, OTPs, exam catalogs, courses, vocabulary decks, and attempt logs, consistent with the business rules for security and data integrity.
Future releases may introduce progressive enhancements for mobile friendliness and potentially dedicated mobile apps if justified by usage patterns. Any significant infrastructure changes required for scaling (load balancing, database replication, backup strategy) must be planned before large-scale exam campaigns. Short training materials (e.g., short videos or guides) will be prepared to help learners and admins understand how to register, take exams, enroll in courses, and manage content using Langfens English.
